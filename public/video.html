Why train the neck and shoulder together?

The movement pattern behind the program.

Some neck and shoulder patterns involve more work from the surface muscles and less contribution from the deep neck and shoulder-blade muscles.

This program uses Janda’s upper-crossed model to organize practice into three clear phases: reduce excess effort, build control, and integrate the movement.

Use the response as feedback. Practice within a comfortable range, then reassess motion, effort, and symptoms.

How the session uses the model

01 / REDUCE EXCESS EFFORT

Make room for easier movement.

Explore: suboccipitals, upper trapezius, levator scapulae, and pectorals.

Reduce unnecessary effort first so the next movement is easier to learn.
02 / BUILD LOCAL CONTROL

Practice deep-neck-flexor control.

Train: a small, controlled head-and-neck movement without dominant superficial-muscle substitution.

Use a small, comfortable range instead of forcing the chin backward.
03 / INTEGRATE THE SYSTEM

Connect the neck to scapular support.

Integrate: lower and middle trapezius, serratus anterior, and comfortable shoulder movement.

Carry that neck control into shoulder-blade and arm movement.

Why technique matters

Knowing what to do is one thing. Knowing how to achieve it is a completely different story.

This session teaches a highly effective active self-myofascial release (SMR) technique designed to inhibit target muscles. It combines direct pressure on tight areas with active movement of the underlying joint and tissue to break up adhesions and improve mobility.

01 / INHIBIT

Reduce unnecessary tension

Use targeted techniques for muscles that are working harder than needed.

02 / ACTIVATE

Recruit the deep neck flexors

Learn to activate the deep neck flexors without substituting larger surface muscles.

03 / INTEGRATE

Connect neck and scapular control

Bring the lower trapezius into the pattern to support scapular stability and coordinated posture.
